Project Overview & Step-by-Step Research Roadmap

Simple Guide: What We Did, Why, and What We Found
1. What Is the Project Statement?

Predict the probability that a 0.25° × 0.25° (~27.8 km) forest area in India will have at least one forest fire in the next 72 hours (3 days), using only weather and fire data known up to 23:59 IST today.

2. Why Is There a Need?

Satellites only detect fires after they start burning, and they mix up destructive forest fires with farmers burning leftover crop stalks (20.5% of detections). Forest officers need a 3-day early warning specifically for forests.

3. Where Did We Get the Data?

• ERA5 Weather Archive (1947–2026): 39 raw meteorological files (3.94M records of temperature, humidity, wind, rain, sunlight).
• NASA FIRMS Satellites (2000–2025): 2.81M satellite fire detections from MODIS (1 km) & VIIRS (375 m).

4. How Did We Process It?

Converted UTC satellite/weather times to Indian Standard Time (IST, UTC+05:30), converted accumulated rainfall/sunlight into daily totals, removed 70.1K duplicate weather records, and removed 590.7K duplicate satellite passes (leaving 2.22M unique fires).

5. What Analysis Did We Do?

Separated all 2.22M fires into Forest Fires (47.2%), Farm Crop Burning (20.5%), and Uncertain/Mixed (32.4%); computed physical air dryness (VPD) and fire-weather indices (FFWI); and analyzed 74-year Indian climate trends.

6. What Measures Did We Take?

• Split data by year: 2003–2011 Train, 2012–2015 Validation, 2016–2025 Test.
• Added a 3-day embargo gap (skipping 3 buffer days between splits so a 3-day forecast window at the end of Training never overlaps into Validation/Test).
• Never filled missing data with fake zeros.

7. What Models Did We Build?

Compared 9 models across 3 groups:
• 2 Simple Baselines: Seasonal Average & 7-Day Recent Fire Memory
• 4 Tabular ML Models: Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM
• 3 Deep Learning Models: ConvLSTM, ConvGRU, U-Net

8. What Is the Final Result?

• ConvGRU (Best Overall): Achieved 0.8028 PR-AUC (+81.8% better than the Seasonal Baseline 0.4416).
• Calibrated LightGBM: Catches 63.8% of real forest fires while raising false alarms on only 1.44% of quiet days.

Plain-English Glossary of Abbreviations & Key Terms Used on This Page:
• Embargo Gap (3-Day Buffer): Skipping 3 days at the boundary between Train, Validation, and Test sets so the 72-hour (3-day) target window of the last Training day cannot leak into Validation.
• LOEO (Leave-One-Ecoregion-Out): Training the model on 7 Indian forest regions and testing it on the 8th completely unseen region to prove it works in new geography.
• PR-AUC (Precision–Recall Area Under Curve): Primary score (0 to 1) for rare events like fires; measures how well the model catches fires without false alarms.
• VPD (Vapor Pressure Deficit): How dry the air is (in kPa). High VPD sucks moisture out of forest leaves and dead wood, making them easy to ignite.
• FFWI (Fosberg Fire Weather Index): Standard forestry index (0 to 100) combining temperature, humidity, and wind speed to measure fire spread danger.
• Brier Score & ECE (Expected Calibration Error): Measure whether a predicted "30% fire risk" actually results in fire 30% of the time (closer to 0 is better).

Unit & Target (Y = 1)

Prediction-Time Cutoff

Issued at 23:59 IST on day D using only weather & fire history up to day D (t ≤ D). Zero future weather leakage.

Scope & Claim Limits

Predicts regional forest flammability + active fire persistence at 28 km scale—not individual human sparks or small sub-canopy fires.

2. Data Understanding, Preprocessing & Fire Attribution

Criteria 2 & 3 • 20 Pts

What One Row Represents (8.92M Forest Grid-Days)

  • Unit: One 0.25° × 0.25° Indian forest cell on date D (450,000 balanced sample rows: 150K Train, 150K Validation, 150K Test).
  • Weather up to Day D: Temp (t2m), Dewpoint (d2m), Humidity (rh), Air Dryness (vpd_max_kpa), Wind (wind10), Solar Radiation (ssrd), Fire Weather Index (ffwi_max).
  • Drought & Fire Memory up to Day D: Past 3/7/14/30-day rainfall totals, consecutive dry days, and past 1/3/7-day active fire counts.
  • Land Cover & Season: Forest fraction, crop fraction, day-of-year sine/cosine, and forest ecoregion.

Data Quality Checks & Anti-Leakage Safeguards

  • Duplicate File Removal: Detected that data_5.grib and data_6.grib were 100% identical (35,064 solar records) and removed 70,135 overlapping records.
  • Grid Alignment: Aligned 4 slightly different GRIB grid sizes (119×125 to 125×125) onto one exact 0.25° India grid.
  • Honest Missing-Data Handling: Left the 1997–2008 wind gap as NaN (missing) rather than filling fake 0.0 wind speeds.
  • 3-Day Embargo Gap (Split Buffer): Skipped the final 3 days before each split boundary so a 3-day prediction window [D+1..D+3] at the end of Training never overlaps into Validation or Test.

8. Error Analysis, Limitations, Defense Q&A & Reproducibility

Criteria 6, 7 & 10 • 20 Pts

Why False Positives Occur (1.44% False Alarm Rate)

2,005 false alarms (out of 138,798 non-fire days at threshold θ = 0.324) happen during hot, dry April–May weeks (air dryness VPD > 4 kPa) when forests are dry and ready to burn, but no human or lightning spark occurred in those 3 days.

Why False Negatives Occur (36.2% Missed Fires)

4,056 missed fire days happen mostly on sudden first-day ignitions (lag7d == 0, no fire in the past week) under mild weather or partial cloud cover in Northeast India and the Himalayas.

Satellite & Unseen-Region (LOEO) Limits

• Heavy monsoon/winter clouds or smoke can hide small low-heat surface fires.
• MODIS satellite (2003–2011) has 1 km pixels vs. VIIRS 375 m (2012+).
• Eastern Himalayas drops to 0.329 in Leave-One-Ecoregion-Out (LOEO) because high-altitude forests burn at much cooler temperatures (15–20 °C).

Viva / Defense Reasoning: "What Changes Under a Small Modification?"

Q1: Raise forest cover threshold from 30% to 40%?

Attributed forest-fire share changes by only −1.89% (47.16% → 45.27%), proving our fire labels sit inside core forests rather than noisy border pixels.

Q2: Remove the 3-day embargo gap (split buffer) or use a random split?

Without the 3-day buffer gap (embargo), the 3-day target window at the end of Training overlaps into Validation/Test, and random splits leak shared multi-day heatwaves—artificially inflating PR-AUC by +0.05 to +0.08.

Q3: Drop recent fire memory (Weather-Only Forecast)?

Removing 1–7 day recent fire memory lowers LightGBM PR-AUC by −0.0369 (to 0.7561), which still beats the Seasonal Climatology baseline (0.4416) by +71.2%.
